__Drafting Notes__

__1.	__Post the IBBI (Liquidation Process) (Fourth Amendment) Regulations, 2026 (w.e.f. 01.06.2026), the outer limit under Regulation 44(1) is 180 days (earlier one year). For liquidations commenced before the relevant amendment, the timeline as it stood on the liquidation commencement date applies — verify and adjust paragraph 3 accordingly.

__2.	__Consultation committee — mandatory step, advice not binding on merits: under the amended Regulation 44(2), the continuation proposal must be placed before the consultation committee (SCC) before filing, and this consultation is a procedural precondition to be evidenced on record (Annexure A-3). The SCC’s advice is, however, recommendatory and does not bind the liquidator on the merits; where the liquidator’s view differs from the SCC’s advice, he should record the reasons for the same. (Contrast the early-dissolution route, where the liquidator applies along with a report of the consultation.)

__3.	__Forward caveat — shift from SCC to CoC supervision: the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (Amendment) Act, 2026 inserts Section 21(10) placing the conduct of the liquidation process under the supervision of the Committee of Creditors. This provision is not yet in force (commencement date to be notified by the Central Government). Once notified, references to the “consultation committee” in this workflow are to be read/aligned to the CoC — verify the position in force on the date of filing.

__4.	__The liquidator continues to discharge responsibilities until the application is decided (clarification to Regulation 44).

__5.	__File the application in the prescribed form under Rule 11 / the applicable NCLT Rules with the requisite court fee, affidavit in support, and index of annexures; serve on stakeholders as directed.
