# __1.  The exit route at a glance__

Until this amendment there was no mechanism to reverse a voluntary liquidation once commenced short of dissolution; a corporate person that had entered the process had no pathway to exit even where circumstances changed (for example, a fresh business opportunity making continuation commercially unwarranted). The 2026 amendments close that gap. The mechanism is a members’ resolution (in EGM/AGM), creditor approval where debt is owed, a liquidator’s report to the Adjudicating Authority, and intimation to IBBI and the Registrar of Companies — on which the proceeding is deemed terminated.

__Statutory anchors__

- __Section 59(5A) IBC __— at any time after commencement but before a dissolution application under Section 59(7) is filed, the voluntary liquidation is terminated if: (a) the members pass a special resolution to terminate the proceeding; and (b) where the corporate person owes any debt on the date of that resolution, creditors representing two-thirds in value of such debt approve it within seven days of the resolution.
- __Section 59(5B) IBC __— the liquidator shall intimate the Board (IBBI) and the Registrar of Companies regarding the special resolution within seven days of passing the resolution or subsequent creditor approval, as the case may be.
- __Section 59(5C) IBC __— the proceeding is deemed terminated from the date the liquidator intimates the Registrar of Companies, and such termination brings the term of the liquidator to an end.
- __Regulation 42, IBBI (VL Process) Regulations, 2017 __(inserted w.e.f. 1 June 2026) — the procedural rulebook: 42(1) contents of the termination resolution; 42(2) liquidator’s report to the Adjudicating Authority; 42(3) seven-day intimation to IBBI and RoC; 42(4) consequences for the liquidator.

__  Point to note on forms: __the Second Amendment omitted Schedule I and re-designated the old Schedule II as Schedule I; several prescribed forms are now notified separately by IBBI circular. Use the current IBBI-notified intimation form/e-form for the actual filing — the precedents below track its substantive content.  

# __2.  Step map__

1. __Board meeting __— the liquidator/board records the rationale for terminating the process, confirms solvency, and convenes the general meeting.
2. __General meeting (EGM/AGM) __— members pass a special resolution under Section 59(5A)(a) to terminate the voluntary liquidation, stating reasons, the treatment of liquidation costs, and a declaration that stakeholders’ interests will not be adversely affected (Reg 42(1)).
3. __Creditor approval __— where the corporate person owes debt, obtain approval of creditors representing two-thirds in value of the debt under Section 59(5A)(b), within seven days of the special resolution.
4. __MGT-14 __— file the special resolution with the RoC under Section 117, Companies Act, 2013 {{ WITHIN_30_DAYS }}.
5. __Liquidator’s report to the Adjudicating Authority __— place a report before the NCLT under Reg 42(2) confirming due process, no intent to defraud, and continuing solvency.
6. __Intimation to IBBI and RoC __— within seven days of the special resolution (or creditor approval) under Section 59(5B) and Reg 42(3), in the IBBI-notified form.
7. __Termination takes effect __— the proceeding is deemed terminated from the date of intimation to the RoC (Section 59(5C)); the liquidator’s term ends and no further action lies under the VL framework (Reg 42(4)).
8. __Housekeeping __— close the liquidation bank account and reverse liquidation-specific arrangements; restore the corporate person to going-concern governance; retain records.

# __3.  Who does what — allocation of responsibilities__

This route spans two different actors. The resolution-side instruments belong to the company and its members; the two statutory filings belong to the __appointed liquidator__ — a separate insolvency professional — who makes them in his own name. The company cannot file the report to the Tribunal or the intimation to IBBI and the RoC; the adviser coordinates the sequence.

# __4.  When this route is not available__

__Hard legal gates — the route simply does not apply__

- __Loss of solvency. __The liquidator must confirm the corporate person remains solvent and able to meet liabilities (Reg 42(2)). If solvency has slipped during the liquidation, this route is closed — a different pathway applies.
- __After a dissolution application. __Section 59(5A) operates only before an application under Section 59(7) is filed. Once dissolution is sought/ordered, there is nothing left to terminate.
- __No special resolution. __Failing the 75% members’ majority defeats it at the threshold.
- __No creditor approval where debt is owed. __Under s.59(5A)(b), a hold-out that drops you below two-thirds in value, or delay beyond seven days, blocks it.
- __Any defrauding purpose. __The liquidator must state the termination is not intended to defraud any person; a reversal aimed at defeating a creditor or a pending claim cannot be certified.
- __Financial service providers __are outside voluntary liquidation altogether, so the route never applies.

__Practical blockers — technically open, but it will not work cleanly__

- __Distributions already made. __Once assets are realised and paid out, clawback is difficult and the “stakeholders not adversely affected” declaration becomes untenable; advanced-stage liquidations are effectively out.
- __Undischarged or disputed claims. __Pending claims or litigation sit poorly with the declaration; the claims position (Reg 28A/29) should be clean first.
- __LLP client. __This pack is drafted for a company (EGM, s.102, MGT-14). For an LLP it becomes a designated-partners’ resolution and LLP filings — the instruments must be adapted.
- __Cannot resume as a going concern. __Registrations must be live, no strike-off initiated and statutory filings current — otherwise the reversal is hollow.
- __Contested termination. __Termination is deemed on RoC intimation, but if a stakeholder objects the Tribunal can still be drawn in despite the report being framed as intimation-for-record.

__  Timing / transitional: __because the statute allows termination “any time after commencement but before a dissolution application is filed,” the route is available for a liquidation that commenced before 1 June 2026, provided no dissolution application has yet been filed. Confirm against the commencement clause of the Second Amendment Regulations for the specific matter.
