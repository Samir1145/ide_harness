# __Note — Interim Moratorium under Section 96 (as amended)__

Section 96 of the Code provides for an interim moratorium that, in the ordinary case, commences on the filing of an application under Section 94 or Section 95 and continues until admission or rejection under Section 100. By the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (Amendment) Act, 2026, a new sub-section (Section 96(4)) has been inserted with effect from 26 May 2026 to provide that the interim moratorium under Section 96 shall not be available where the application is for initiating the insolvency resolution process in respect of a personal guarantor to a{{ CORPORATE_DEBTOR_NAME }}.

The legislative purpose, as recorded in the materials preceding the amendment, is to prevent a personal guarantor from filing an application merely to secure the benefit of the interim moratorium and thereby stall recovery proceedings. Accordingly, the precedents below do not plead the protection of an interim moratorium in a personal-guarantor matter; the moratorium proper under Section 101 arises only on admission under Section 100.

*To confirm before filing: the exact text and numbering of Section 96(4) against the gazette, and any consequential change to the corresponding interim-moratorium provision in the bankruptcy chapter (Section 124).*

# __Drafting Notes__

•	These are template precedents for the pre-admission stage of a personal-guarantor matter under Chapter III of Part III — the initiation applications under Sections 94 and 95 and the resolution professional’s report under Section 99 — leading to the order of admission or rejection under Section 100.

•	Forum: the default forum is the NCLT New Delhi Bench; where a CIRP or liquidation of the{{ CORPORATE_DEBTOR_NAME }}is pending, the personal-guarantor matter is filed before the bench seized of that corporate process.

•	Fee: the initiation application under Section 94 / 95 carries a fee of Rs. 2,000/- under the Application (PG to CD) Rules, 2019, in Form A / B / C respectively.

•	Interim moratorium: by Section 96(4) (effective 26 May 2026) the interim moratorium under Section 96 is not available in a personal-guarantor matter; do not plead it. The moratorium under Section 101 arises on admission.

•	Resolution professional’s report: the Section 99 report is now to be submitted within twenty-one days (substituted for ten days) of the appointment; service of the report tracks Section 99(10) as amended.

The Code-level position reflects the IBC (Amendment) Act, 2026 in force with effect from 26 May 2026 (MCA S.O. 2625(E) dated 22 May 2026); regulation- and rule-level vintages are to be confirmed.*
