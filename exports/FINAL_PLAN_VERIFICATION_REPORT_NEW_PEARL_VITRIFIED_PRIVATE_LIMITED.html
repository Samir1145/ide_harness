Section 6 (Contd.): Institutional Legal Fine Print & Diligence Boundaries THE 12 MANDATORY STATUTORY DISCLAIMERS

While utmost professional care and rigorous verification standards have been exercised in executing this statutory plan audit, the use, interpretation, and legal reliance on this dossier are subject to the following statutory caveats, institutional limitations, and terms of engagement:

1. Factual & Statutory Verification vs. Commercial Wisdom of CoC: This report evaluates the statutory conformity of the Resolution Plan under IBC Section 30(2) and CIRP Regulation 38. The economic viability, feasibility, commercial haircut tolerance, and distribution percentages across creditor classes remain within the sole commercial wisdom of the Committee of Creditors as affirmed by the Supreme Court in CoC of Essar Steel India Ltd. v. Satish Kumar Gupta (2019).
2. Reliance on Registered Valuer Reports for Liquidation Benchmark: Verification of minimum statutory entitlements under Section 30(2)(b) and Section 53 waterfall relies upon the Fair Value and Liquidation Value determined by the independent registered valuers appointed by the Resolution Professional. Resolution Bazaar does not independently execute physical asset appraisals or real estate valuations.
3. Conditionality & Judicial Waiver Boundaries: Reliefs, concessions, and waivers requested in Chapter 7 are treated strictly as prayers before the Adjudicating Authority (NCLT) and not conditions precedent. In accordance with the Supreme Court doctrine in Ebix Singapore Pte Ltd. v. CoC of Educomp Solutions Ltd. (2021), the resolution plan cannot be conditional; failure of NCLT to grant discretionary waivers shall not vitiate the binding nature of the plan.
4. Proof of Funds & Net Worth Certificate Standard: Financial feasibility and implementation capability under Regulation 38(3) are verified against audited Chartered Accountant net worth certificates, bank solvency letters, and certified financial statements submitted by the RA. Unencumbered availability of credit lines remains subject to definitive banking covenants.
5. Beneficial Ownership & Regulation 38(3A) Disclosures: Under IBBI Regulation 38(3A) and Circular IBBI/CIRP/90/2025, ultimate beneficial ownership identifying natural persons controlling the RA is verified against applicant disclosures and statutory MCA filings. Undisclosed beneficial arrangements not discoverable through public registries cannot be independently inferred.
6. Section 32A Clean Slate Demarcation: Immunity from pre-admission offences under Section 32A applies strictly upon approval and handover of management to a completely unrelated acquirer pursuant to Ghanashyam Mishra and Sons Pvt. Ltd. v. Edelweiss ARC (2021) and M.K. Rajagopalan (2023). Ongoing promoter nexus negates statutory immunity.
7. Avoidance Proceedings Prosecution & Pass-Through Mechanics: Analysis under Regulation 38(1A) confirms that avoidance applications filed under Sections 43, 45, 50, and 66 survive plan approval per Tata Steel BSL. The ultimate quantum of recovery remains subject to judicial decrees; this report certifies structural assignment and pass-through compliance.
8. Third-Party & Personal Guarantor Non-Discharge: Extinguishment of Corporate Debtor liabilities under the plan does not discharge or release third-party corporate or personal guarantors under the Supreme Court doctrine in Lalit Kumar Jain v. Union of India (2021). Lenders retain full statutory rights of independent recovery.
9. Regulatory & Antitrust Approvals (CCI, SEBI, Telecom): Statutory approvals required under Regulation 38(2)(c) (including Competition Commission of India combination clearances, Department of Telecommunications licenses, and SEBI delisting exemptions) must be secured by the RA post-approval; this report verifies disclosure adequacy.
10. Good Faith Information Standard: Resolution Bazaar has executed diligence based on documents, affidavits, and information provided by the RP and RA. In the absence of evidence of fraud or wilful suppression, Resolution Bazaar assumes no liability for incorrect or misleading data furnished by the Resolution Applicant.
11. Temporal Cut-Off Date & No Continuing Monitoring Duty: Findings, audit badges, and statutory certifications are valid strictly as of the Report Date (11 September 2026). Resolution Bazaar undertakes no ongoing monitoring or obligation to update this dossier with events occurring subsequent to the Report Date.
