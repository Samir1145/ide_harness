# Asf Buildtech Pvt Ltd Vs. Shapoorji Pallonji And Company Pvt Ltd
* **Citation:** (2025) SC
* **Court:** before the referral court
* **Date:** 2025-05-01
* **Parties:** which is completely against the dictum of Oil and Natural Gas Corporation Ltd v. Discovery Enterprises Pvt

### Headnote / Legal Dictum
HeadNote Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 – Sections 2(1)(h) , 7 , 11 , 16 , 21 , 23 , 37 – Impleadment of Non-Signatories – The Supreme Court, after an exhaustive review of statutory provisions, judicial precedents, and the evolution of the law, holds that an arbitral tribunal possesses the authority and power to implead or join a non-signatory to the arbitration agreement as a party to the arbitration proceedings, even if such non-signatory was not a party before the referral court under Section 11 or was not issued a notice of invocation under Section 21. The Court clarifies that the power to implead non-signatories is grounded in Sections 2(1)(h) and 7, and is exercised under Section 16 (kompetenz-kompetenz), and that the doctrine of ‘group of companies’ and other consent-based principles may be applied by the tribunal. The requirement of notice under Section 21 is procedural and not jurisdictional. The appeal challenging the High Court’s order upholding the arbitral tribunal’s impleadment of a non-signatory is dismissed .

### Case Summary
The Supreme Court has ruled that an arbitral tribunal can implead a non-signatory to the arbitration agreement as a party to the arbitration proceedings, provided they meet certain criteria outlined in the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996. This decision builds upon previous judicial interpretations and the evolution of the law surrounding the inclusion of non-signatories in arbitration cases.

## Facts

- The case involves Asf Buildtech Pvt Ltd versus Shapoorji Pallonji and Company Pvt Ltd.
- It pertains to the application of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996.
- The dispute centers around the inclusion of a non-signatory in arbitration proceedings.
- The Supreme Court reviewed various sections of the Act, including Sections 2(1)(h), 7, 11, 16, 21, 23, 37, and 38.
- The Court held that an arbitral tribunal has the authority to implead a non-signatory to the arbitration agreement, provided it meets specific conditions.
- The decision upholds the tribunal's power to do so under Sections 2(1)(h) and 7, and emphasizes its competence under Section 16 (kompetenz-kompetenz).
- The requirement of notice under Section 21 is deemed procedural rather than jurisdictional.
- The appeal against the High Court’s order was dismissed.

## Key Principle

The key principle established by the Supreme Court in this case is that an arbitral tribunal has the authority to implead a non-signatory to the arbitration agreement as a party to the arbitration proceedings, provided the non-signatory meets the specified criteria outlined in the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996. This ruling underscores the flexibility and adaptability of arbitration procedures, allowing tribunals to address complex disputes involving multiple parties while ensuring fairness and efficiency.
