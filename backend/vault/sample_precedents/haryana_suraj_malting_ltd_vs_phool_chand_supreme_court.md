# Haryana Suraj Malting Ltd Vs. Phool Chand
* **Citation:** (2018) SC
* **Court:** IN THE SUPREME COURT
* **Date:** 2018-05-17
* **Parties:** Hans Raj 1 and Radhakrishna Mani Tripathi v. L

### Headnote / Legal Dictum
HeadNote Labour Court/Industrial Tribunal retains power to set aside ex parte award even after award becomes enforceable, subject to sufficient cause and reasonable time Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 – Sections 11, 17, 17A, 20, 29, 38; Industrial Disputes (Central) Rules, 1957 – Rules 10B(9), 22, 24; Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 – Order IX Rule 13 – Industrial adjudication – Ex parte award – Functus officio – Recall/setting aside after enforceability – Core issue whether Tribunal loses power after award becomes enforceable under S. 17A – Scheme of Act and Central Rules permitting ex parte procedure subject to sufficient cause – Grindlays Bank principle of ancillary/incidental powers and procedural review ex debito justitiae – HELD : Tribunal’s power to set aside an ex parte award is founded on natural justice and ancillary/incidental powers, guided by broad principles of Order IX Rule 13; it is not circumscribed by the 30-day enforceability period – management must show sufficient cause and approach within reasonable time; Tribunal to exercise judicious discretion and balance equities – Awards remitted to Labour Court to consider sufficient cause; appellants directed to make provisional payment of Rs.1,00,000/- in each case, adjustable to final outcome.
