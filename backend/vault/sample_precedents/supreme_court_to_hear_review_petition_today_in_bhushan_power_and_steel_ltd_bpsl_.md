### Full Text
A Supreme Court Bench comprising Hon’ble the Chief Justice and Hon’ble Mr. Justice Satish Chandra Sharma is set to hear a review petition today, challenging its landmark order delivered on May 2, 2025, reported at Kalyani Transco v. Bhushan Power and Steel Ltd. and Ors., (2025) SC in the high-profile insolvency case involving Bhushan Power and Steel Ltd. (BPSL). The original order had quashed the resolution plan approved for BPSL and directed the NCLT to initiate liquidation proceedings. The review petition, led by Punjab National Bank and other stakeholders including operational creditors and erstwhile promoters, will be heard in open court at 3:00 p.m., as per the orders of the Chief Justice of India and Justice Satish Chandra Sharma. The Supreme Court had earlier allowed applications for open court hearing and oral arguments, issuing notices to all concerned parties. Background of the Case: The insolvency proceedings against BPSL were triggered in 2017 at the behest of Punjab National Bank under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016. The Committee of Creditors (CoC) eventually approved a consolidated resolution plan submitted by JSW Steel, after evaluating competing bids from Tata Steel and Liberty House. However, the process was marred by criminal investigations, with both the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) and Enforcement Directorate (ED) launching cases related to alleged fraud and money laundering. The Supreme Court, in its May 2, 2025, reported at Kalyani Transco v. Bhushan Power and Steel Ltd. and Ors., (2025) SC , judgment, found multiple lapses in the conduct of the resolution process. The Court held that the Resolution Professional (RP) and the CoC had failed to strictly adhere to the mandatory requirements of the IBC and its regulations, including time limits, eligibility checks, and compliance certifications. The apex court also ruled that the NCLT and NCLAT had overstepped their jurisdiction in entertaining certain appeals and granting reliefs, particularly in matters involving public law and the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA). Significantly, the Court quashed the approval of JSW Steel’s resolution plan, ordered initiation of liquidation proceedings against BPSL, and set aside the judgments of both the NCLT and NCLAT. The judgment also emphasized that commercial wisdom of the CoC is not absolute and must comply with statutory requirements, and found the conduct of JSW and the CoC to be dubious and contrary to the intent of the IBC. Review Petitions &amp; What’s at Stake: The order has far-reaching implications for banks, investors, and insolvency professionals, particularly regarding the timelines for corporate insolvency resolution and the boundaries of judicial review by insolvency tribunals. Petitioners in the review have raised concerns over the potential loss to creditors and stakeholders from liquidation, the interpretation of mandatory timelines, and the impact on bona fide resolution applicants. The matters listed today include multiple review petitions by banks, operational creditors, and other parties, all challenging various aspects of the Supreme Court’s order. Source: Kalyani Transco v. Bhushan Power and Steel Ltd. and Ors., (2025) SC
